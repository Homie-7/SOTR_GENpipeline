## DESIGNED FOR DESTRUCTION (added on Homie's note)
The deck we land on is the ship that grinds, tips, loses its mast and becomes the raft. So:
- **Two layers per wall: the SHIP (rails, shrouds, mast, deck) and the SEA + SKY behind it.** Built by keying the
  generated plate (method chosen by a free test in IMG). Then the horizon can tilt behind a level ship ("tipped over
  on side", as it really looks from on board), the sea can shallow and brighten, the rowboats sit on the sea layer, the
  light can change per state, and the ship can break away to open sea (the dissolve into the raft).
- **One ship, one text block** (the clone rule): the same frigate, rigging and colours in the flight, on deck and in
  the wreck. Its sources: Baugean's Medusa (S6 public domain), the pack's period paintings (Diemer, Monamy).
- **The mainmast lives whole on CENTRE**; its shrouds on LEFT and RIGHT run up out of the TOP edge to the masthead,
  never across a seam. It falls **forward and away, into depth** (S6 "depth, not width"), so nothing crosses a seam.
  On the side walls the shrouds go slack when it falls (optional extra; dropped if hard).
- **Each seam is covered by a structural post of the ship** (a bulwark stanchion or a deadeye channel), so the sea
  layers of neighbouring walls never have to meet exactly.
- **The dissolve into the raft is historically right:** the real raft was built from the Méduse's own masts, yards and
  planks. So the ship comes apart along its planks and spars (wood breaks like wood, LOOK D16, never cubes) and drifts
  off, leaving open sea on all three walls around the physical raft.
- **One side-wall sea loop, mirrored, serves both LEFT and RIGHT** (the water runs past each side the opposite way, as
  it should). It halves the sea-loop cost.

## THE PLAN, in priority order (credits spent top-down; stop where they run out)

### 1. THE INTRO — the goéland brings us onto the Medusa (finished quality)
**Where it plays (rec):** Scene 7, at the hoist: "The music swells as gigantic sails are hoisted… we are brought 'onto'
the ship." **~45 s** (the 2022 cut ran 2:30). **No title, no shark, no "to be continued"** (Scene 7 is mid-show).
1. **Open** on the Medusa small on the horizon under sail, bright day: the same image Scene 6 ends on ("The Medusa
   appears on the horizon"), so Scene 6 hands straight into it.
2. **A goéland argenté** (herring gull: white head and body, pale grey back, black wingtips with white spots, yellow
   bill with a red spot, pink legs; photoreal, replacing the 2022 bird, per "bird change") glides in low over the swell
   and flies toward the ship; the camera follows it.
3. The ship grows: a 1816 French frigate, three masts, sails set (outside, the sails are right; they ARE the physical
   hoist), the white Bourbon ensign at the stern.
4. The gull rises over the stern rail and lands on the rail; its wing sweeps the lens = the cut.
5. **Land on the deck POV, all three walls** (§2), the gull perched on the rail on one side wall, then off.

**Walls during the flight (rec):** the flight is a window on CENTRE (16:9 cropped to 5:3); LEFT and RIGHT hold the same
day sea and sky as slow ambient loops, so the room is "at sea" while the bird travels on the back wall. A moving
camera can't be split across three generated walls (they can't be synced), and nothing crosses a seam.
**Made:** NBP keyframes (gull ref; the ship from Baugean's Medusa engraving, already in `S6_public_domain/`, + the
Diemer painting) → Seedance image-to-video, 4 s probe, then the flight as one take + a Sequel for the landing (a native
join, as the salon snuff). Sound on (house rule).

### 2. THE DECK — "the final perspective", no sails (the canvas for everything after)

- Deck master (NBP 21:9, never projected) → CENTRE (16:9) → LEFT, RIGHT (4:3), refs master + CENTRE (PIPELINE 2-5;

  "smooth fast → smooth slow" change, the roll, the TILT ("tipped over on side"), the SHUDDER and the "zoom more" are
  done by script on the locked plates (exact, repeatable, 0 cr).
